## Rules Reference

There currently does not exist a Living Rules Reference section regarding coexistence.

## Notes

- Some game effects allow two or more players’ units to exist on a planet without triggering combat; this is coexistence.
- One player controls the planet; all other players are coexisting on that planet.
    - If a game effect instructs a player to coexist on a planet they do not control, the original controller of that planet retains control.
    - If a game effect instructs a player to coexist on a planet they do control, then another player will gain control of that planet (which will exhaust that planet).
- If multiple players may choose to coexist on a planet during the same timing window, the active player chooses first; if they decline, the other player may then choose to coexist.
- If additional units are added to those already coexisting, by, for example, committing additional ground forces during an invasion, or resolving an effect that allows a unit to be placed directly on a planet, the added units immediately coexist.
- Structures without ground forces may coexist on a planet by themselves, and may maintain control of a planet while another player’s ground forces coexist on it.
- Structure limits apply to each player separately. A planet in coexistence may contain up to two PDS and one space dock belonging to each player.
- If only one player has units on a planet that was in coexistence, and that player was coexisting, they gain control of that planet.
    - Outside of the **Invasion** step of a tactical action, this happens immediately, such as when the controlling player moves all of their units off that planet.
    - During an invasion, if the last of the controlling player’s units on a planet are destroyed, such as by `Bombardment`, control does not pass to the coexisting player until the **Establish Control** step. The invading player may land ground forces and gain control of the planet, and the coexisting player remains in coexistence.
    - A player who enters coexistence on a planet they control when ground forces are committed to it may still use `Space Cannon` against those ground forces during the **Space Cannon Defense** step. They do not lose control of the planet until the **Establish Control** step, and if they are then the only player with units on the planet, they are no longer coexisting and retain control.
    - If multiple players are coexisting on a planet and the player that controls that planet no longer has units on it, control passes to the next coexisting player in the order used to resolve abilities in the current phase: initiative order during the action phase, otherwise clockwise order.
- All coexisting units are blockaded, regardless of what ships, if any, are in the system. This includes units that are not structures, such as mechs with the `Production` ability.
- Coexisting units may use their `Production` ability to produce ground forces, but cannot produce ships.
- A unit with `Planetary Shield` on a planet protects all units on that planet, including those of the controlling player and coexisting players. All PDS on a planet, including coexisting PDS, may use `Space Cannon` during the **Space Cannon Defense** step against ground forces committed by the active player.
- When a player uses the `Bombardment` ability of 1 of their units against a planet in coexistence, they choose which player’s units on that planet will have the hits assigned.
    - If that player has multiple units with the `Bombardment` ability, they make this choice independently for each unit.
    - If that player produces a surplus of hits above the number of units of the player they have chosen, the extra hits cannot be assigned to the units of a different player on that planet.
- A player who controls a planet may start a ground combat against a coexisting player during the invasion step of a tactical action.
- A player who is coexisting on a planet may start a ground combat against the player that controls that planet during the invasion step of a tactical action.
- A player who had no units on a planet, but commits 1 or more ground forces during the invasion step of a tactical action must start a ground combat against the player that controls that planet (unless they have an ability that would allow them to coexist).
- Should a player win the ground combat on a planet resulting from one of these aforementioned methods, they gain control of that planet, if they were not in control of it already. Then, they may start an additional ground combat against another coexisting player, if present, and may continue to do so until they decline to start another ground combat or there are no more coexisting players. Any coexisting players that they do not resolve a ground combat against remain coexisting.
- When a player ends their coexistence by starting a ground combat, all of their units on that planet, including structures, stop coexisting, even if they do not win the ground combat.
- When scoring an objective, a coexisting player considers both themselves and the controlling player to control that planet. The player that controls the planet does not count a coexisting player as also controlling it.
    - A player may score public objectives even if they coexist on, but do not control, each planet in their home system.
- A player must control a planet, not merely coexist on it, to explore it.
- A player cannot spend another player’s coexisting units to pay the cost of an exploration card, such as *Volatile Fuel Source*.
